Finish the coach-to-client experience, one feature at a time.

Confirmed scope

Session logging and completion timing belong in the training logbook. Do not ask the client to enter the session again in the daily tracker.

  • Coach-assigned targets for cardio, steps and other suitable tracker metrics are a high priority. Cardio is always prescribed as sessions per week x minutes per session, for example 5 x 20 min per week, and shown that way in the weekly timeline.
  • Activity targets have a separate planning destination, not a section under Goals. Steps can be prescribed as minimum, maximum, range or fixed. Changes take effect from the day they are saved, including midweek changes.
  • A minimal supplement schedule with time, product, instruction and status belongs in the immediate frontend scope.
  • The client-side frontend, especially the training logbook, needs priority over more coach-editor polish.
  • Image comparison is an immediate frontend feature, not something to postpone until a paid Posing Studio module.
  • IIFYM needs calorie/macro logging in the daily tracker. Meal-plan clients need a way to report plan accuracy.
  • Session logging and completion timing belong in the training logbook. Do not ask the client to enter the session again in the daily tracker.
  • Menses, peaking and show-day tools remain later add-ons.

Build pipeline

Each slice carries both coach and client screens together. Step 0 is a small enabler, not a multi-week project.

Step 0

Prepare a small, shared client-demo foundation

Short enabling step

Depends on: Existing assigned-tool preview and shared controls.
Why: Every priority feature needs a client/date context and a place to show it. Keep this preparation small; it must not become a new architecture project.

Coach screens

  • Keep the existing roster, template assignment and plan editors as the coach entry points.
  • Provide an explicit preview switch between coach and fictional client views. Do not build a fake login or imply authenticated access.

Client screens

  • Reuse the assigned-tool preview as a navigable client workspace with clear current date and enabled sections.
  • Define mobile navigation and back/resume behavior before the logbook grows. Put supplements and progress photos in discoverable destinations; final navigation labels remain a design decision.

Build structure

  • Introduce a thin shared demo-state adapter for the selected fictional client and date, rather than separate sample records inside every new screen.
  • Keep template configuration, assigned prescriptions and recorded actuals separate. Reuse stable client, exercise, set, metric and plan-version IDs.
  • Prepare a few reusable scenarios: no plan, an active plan, incomplete day/session, previous history, a changed future prescription and unavailable media. Provide a reset-demo action.

Completion test

Changing between coach and client preview shows the same fictional assignment and date; navigating away and back does not accidentally reset an in-progress demo. No backend is required.

Not now

No new global state framework, account system, production persistence, full navigation redesign or wholesale component-folder migration.

Step 1

Build Activity targets planning and the client target display

First priority feature

Depends on: Step 0, existing client-setup navigation and shared controls. The Goals screen remains separate.
Why: This is the smallest useful coach-to-client slice and establishes prescription display patterns reused by the tracker and roadmap.

Coach screens

  • Create a separate Activity targets planning destination in individual client setup, not under Goals. For steps, choose Minimum, Maximum, Range or Fixed with the appropriate value or bounds in steps/day.
  • For cardio, enter sessions per week and duration in minutes per session, for example 5 x 20 min per week. The period is always one week; do not add a period selector or substitute a weekly-minutes-only prescription. Other metrics retain their appropriate daily or weekly meaning.
  • Additions, changes and stops take effect from today's date, including midweek saves. Show active and previous prescriptions; do not require Monday or provide a future-start picker in this first slice.
  • Preserve earlier days when a target changes. Same-day replacement requires confirmation. Validate complete, ordered step ranges without treating a maximum as a minimum.

Client screens

  • Show the applicable target beside the relevant tracker input, with actual and remaining/status wording only when the rule is defined.
  • Provide a small activity/targets overview. Show the cardio prescription as sessions per week x minutes per session in both the client view and weekly timeline, for example 5 x 20 min per week, not as a daily requirement.
  • Keep no target, no entry, zero entered, target met and target exceeded as distinct states. A target can exist without forcing an extra manual tracker entry.

Build structure

  • Create reusable ActivityTargetEditor and TargetSummary components with separate activity-planning state. Preserve existing scalar goals without using them as the new feature's state owner.
  • Represent steps Minimum, Maximum, Range and Fixed explicitly, and retain the cardio frequency/duration pair. Use one day-effective resolver across editor, client and timeline.
  • The timeline keeps coach-selected planning fields. A midweek change produces date-labeled prescription segments within that weekly row, not a rewritten weekly value, a prorated quota or an average of cardio pairs.

Completion test

A coach uses the separate Activity targets destination to save all four steps types and 5 sessions per week x 20 minutes per session. The client view and weekly timeline show 5 x 20 min per week; a Wednesday change applies that day and leaves Monday/Tuesday unchanged.

Not now

Automatic recommendations, universal adherence scores, wearable-derived activity and unrestricted custom calculation builders.

Step 2

Add the minimal supplement schedule

Small complete feature

Depends on: Step 0. Reuse the client-setup and prescription presentation patterns.
Why: The requested four-field schedule is bounded and can be completed without building the later protocol module.

Coach screens

  • Add a Supplements section to individual client setup with add, edit, reorder and remove actions.
  • Each row contains time/timing, product, coach-entered instruction and status. Proposed statuses are active, paused and ended, to confirm before build.
  • Allow useful timing text such as 'with breakfast' as well as clock time. Support clear active/inactive presentation; dates are optional if needed to explain schedule history.

Client screens

  • Display a readable daily schedule in the client's plans or supplements view, with product and instruction legible on mobile.
  • Show an empty/not assigned state and distinguish an inactive item from an item the client did not take.
  • Do not add daily supplement adherence logging by default. Ask whether the first version is read-only or needs a simple taken/not-taken action.

Build structure

  • Use a dedicated supplement schedule view model, not general notes or medication/PED columns disguised as supplements.
  • Keep instructions as coach-authored text. Do not generate dosing, interactions, treatment recommendations or cycle logic.
  • Show add/edit confirmation and an undo/remove pattern using shared controls and fictional products.

Completion test

A coach adds and pauses a fictional schedule item; the client preview displays the corresponding active schedule and clear status with all four requested fields.

Not now

Protocols/cycles, sourcing links, supplement analytics, medical decision support and adherence history unless explicitly requested.

Step 3

Build the client training logbook

Main client-side build

Depends on: Step 0 and the existing assigned training prescription. Target/supplement completion is a priority order, not a technical prerequisite.
Why: The program authoring work already exists. The largest missing client interaction is following and recording that program.

Coach screens

  • Reuse current program setup, exercise instructions, rotations and week-based carry-forward rules.
  • Design how a coach reads a completed session and its flagged set note. Provide a future-set instruction state without implementing message delivery.

Client screens

  • Build session selection, session detail, an in-progress session, completed session summary and training history.
  • Place prescribed sets/reps/instructions beside editable actual weight and reps. Include previous-session performance, exercise setup/machine notes and per-set notes.
  • Allow a set note to be flagged for check-in review. Keep the exercise/set/date context visible in both client and coach previews.
  • Support start/resume, mark-set-complete, finish session, edit a prior entry and leave-with-unsaved-changes states. Define skipped/partial sessions rather than converting them into zeros.
  • Record or edit the session's completion date/time in the logbook. The daily tracker may show a read-only training summary but must not require the client to re-enter the session.

Build structure

  • Keep TrainingPrescription read-only and add a separate logging layer keyed to session, exercise, set and prescription version.
  • Use touch-friendly set rows, suitable numeric keyboards and stable focus. Test long exercise names, optional fields, empty history and interrupted sessions.
  • Show a historical prescription alongside its recorded actuals, so changing the coach's future program does not change what the earlier session screen says.
  • Treat RIR/RPE, bodyweight/unilateral logging, supersets and rest timers as explicit choices, not silently assumed first-release requirements.

Completion test

A fictional client opens an assigned workout, records sets, leaves a flagged note, resumes an unfinished session and views the completed session/history. The coach can open the same demo set context.

Not now

Exercise video hosting, device connections, automatic progression recommendations and advanced session tools until their frontend need is confirmed.

Step 4

Build progress-photo capture and comparison

Independent high-priority feature

Depends on: Step 0 plus a shared fictional photo-entry shape. Can run alongside step 3; it does not need the logbook to finish.
Why: One comparator can serve the client progress view and coach check-in review. Building them separately would duplicate the hardest interaction.

Coach screens

  • Add a dated photo gallery reachable from the client profile and check-in review.
  • Choose comparison A/B dates and matching pose/angle labels. Keep the originating check-in context visible.

Client screens

  • Design add/select-photo, date and angle labeling, preview, replace/remove and empty/missing-angle states.
  • Provide a progress gallery and comparison entry point. Confirm whether clients see the same comparator or a simpler gallery.
  • Start with side-by-side comparison, date/angle selectors and zoom/reset. On a narrow screen, provide an explicit A/B toggle or swipeable comparison without relying on hover.

Build structure

  • Use one reusable PhotoGallery and PhotoComparison component in both contexts.
  • Use synthetic or clearly non-client demo assets. Local file preview is sufficient to demonstrate upload interaction; actual media storage is outside scope.
  • Keep image scale/aspect handling consistent, avoid distorted bodies, and make mismatched angle/date selections obvious. Include unavailable-image and replace/remove confirmation states.
  • Do not confuse ordinary progress-image comparison with the later Posing Studio pack.

Completion test

The coach can compare two fictional dated images of the same angle during review; the mobile comparison remains usable; replacing a preview image updates its selected comparison without a backend.

Not now

Annotations/drawing tools, automatic alignment/body analysis, posing routines, advanced overlays and a before/after slider unless specifically useful.

Step 5

Add mode-aware nutrition logging to the tracker

High-priority client reporting

Depends on: Step 0, the target display pattern from step 1 and existing nutrition-plan options.
Why: IIFYM actual intake and meal-plan accuracy answer different questions. They should share a place in the tracker, not one ambiguous field or score.

Coach screens

  • Reuse the existing Meal plan/IIFYM presentation choice and individual target/option setup.
  • Design the selected reporting method for meal-plan accuracy. Do not calculate a percentage or adherence grade until its meaning is agreed.
  • Preserve coach-labeled day options and explain how a manual day-option choice is allowed and displayed.

Client screens

  • For IIFYM, add direct daily consumed kcal, protein, carbs and fat inputs, alongside the applicable targets and a clear not-recorded state.
  • Treat consumed values as actuals, not the macros of prescribed foods. Decide whether calories are independently entered or derived before implementing an editable/derived relationship.
  • For meal plans, show the assigned plan and an accuracy report with optional note. Suggested starting approach is followed/partly followed/not followed at meal level, but this is a proposal to confirm.
  • Do not require both a complete food diary and direct macro entry. Do not make meal-plan clients duplicate their plan foods just to report how closely they followed it.
  • Keep prior entries when switching the visible nutrition approach or date; show only the relevant reporting controls.

Build structure

  • Create NutritionLog with distinct IIFYM actuals and MealPlanAccuracyEntry presentations, rather than treating accuracy as calorie-target compliance.
  • Respect fixed/range/minimum target wording. Show remaining or over-target values only where their interpretation is agreed.
  • Prepare consistency states for partial macro entry, zero entered, manual day-option change, no plan/target and changed prescriptions. Use the same fictional dated entry in tracker and coach review.
  • Keep session selection out of the daily tracker; training date/time comes from the logbook.

Completion test

An IIFYM client can record consumed macros in the tracker; a meal-plan client can report accuracy without entering the same foods again. Coach review shows the relevant report and target context.

Not now

Full searchable food diary, barcode scanning, recipes/favorites and detailed nutrient analysis. These are not prerequisites for this simplified tracker-based reporting pass.

Step 6

Join the priority features in check-in and progress review

Frontend integration pass

Depends on: The new targets, supplement presentation, logbook, comparison and nutrition views.
Why: The handoff needs to demonstrate how the screens relate. Backend absence is acceptable; unrelated mock screens are not enough.

Coach screens

  • Extend the existing check-in review with target/actual context, mode-appropriate nutrition, completed-session/flag links and photo comparison.
  • Show the active supplement schedule if useful, but do not invent supplement adherence statistics when only a read-only schedule was built.
  • Keep open review, draft feedback and answered states distinct. A plan-change preview should link to the intended frontend plan/version.

Client screens

  • Finish full and smaller custom check-in frontend flows: fill, validate, attach fictional visuals, review, submit locally, success and submission history.
  • Show feedback/history and links back to the relevant plan, log or comparison. All submit/delivery outcomes are explicitly demo states.

Build structure

  • Add read-only summaries to dashboard and weekly roadmap using the same sample entries, not another set of unrelated numbers.
  • Use frontend callbacks and fixture transitions to demonstrate the loop. Document future read/write events for the backend partner without implementing those services.
  • Check nutrition-mode changes, selected-date changes, target history, session history and image contexts together.

Completion test

A coherent fictional walkthrough goes from coach setup to client entry, then check-in review and feedback preview without duplicated session logging or contradictory screen values.

Not now

Actual submission processing, real messaging/media transport, authoritative calculations and notifications.

Step 7

Finish remaining core frontend and package the handoff

After the priority slices

Depends on: The priority walkthrough is reviewable and its product decisions are settled.
Why: Close the remaining screen gaps after the requested client workflows, rather than interrupting them with peripheral features.

Coach screens

  • Complete onboarding builder/preview, client creation/invitation states, template variant switching and assignment/update-confirmation screens as needed.
  • Review coach tasks versus client tasks and private versus shared-note presentation. Reuse existing screens rather than rebuild the workspace.

Client screens

  • Finish intake/onboarding, assigned task completion, dated habit interaction and feedback history where still missing.
  • Verify empty/no-assignment navigation and variants with different enabled views. Keep hidden data intact in the demo when presentation changes.

Build structure

  • Create an inventory of screens/components and their ready, empty, loading, validation, failed and success states.
  • Test new shared UI in both modes with pale/dark accents, keyboard input and narrow layouts. Check open popups as well as closed triggers.
  • Deliver a fictional walkthrough, view-model/input/event notes, example fixtures, known decisions and the integration responsibility list. No production-readiness claim is needed.

Completion test

The development partner can inspect the intended UI behavior, reproduce the main demo flows and see exactly where real services will connect. Your frontend deliverable can be accepted independently of backend implementation.

Not now

Wearable connection implementation, real migration/export, paid modules and production infrastructure.

Parallelization notes

  • Default for one builder: 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7. This is a recommended working order, not a claim that every step technically depends on the previous one.
  • With a second independent builder, run photo comparison in step 4 alongside the logbook in step 3 after the shared client/date/media conventions are fixed. Rejoin at step 6.
  • Keep the shared demo-state types and client navigation under one owner. Parallel builders work in separate feature components, not competing edits to model.ts, store.tsx or client-preview.tsx.
  • If capacity is limited, complete a smaller demonstrable slice before adding options. A working set logger and plain side-by-side photo comparison are more useful than many unfinished advanced controls.

Reminders

  • Activity targets belong in their own planning destination, not Goals. Steps supports minimum, maximum, range and fixed; changes apply from the day saved. Weekly timeline rows must retain dated midweek segments.
  • A partial/skipped workout, an unrecorded set and a real zero need different UI states.
  • Machine/setup notes, previous performance and future-set coach instructions are easy to miss when designing the logbook.
  • Photo date/angle labels, missing views, remove/replace behavior and coach/client visibility need a decision alongside the comparison layout.
  • Plan accuracy, off-plan meals and actual macro intake are different concepts; decide which you want rather than collecting all of them by default.
  • Direct IIFYM macro logging is not the same scope as building a full food diary. Keep barcode/recipes out of the immediate critical path.
  • Full/custom client check-in forms still need validation, local submission confirmation, history and feedback screens even when the server is not connected.
  • Client task completion, habit status, general notes and a readable no-plan state should not disappear from the later core-frontend queue.
  • Template/nutrition presentation changes must not discard earlier fictional entries. Keep historical prescription context in the UI.
  • A demo error/loading state is part of frontend completeness. Real retry delivery, access enforcement and synchronization belong to the integration partner.

  1. Decide the ruleResolve only the questions that affect the feature being built. Keep other questions visible without blocking all work.
  2. Design the connected screensCoach setup, client use and coach readout where relevant. Include the narrow layout and empty/partial states.

  4. Exercise the actual UIKeyboard and touch-sized input, optional fields, back/resume, date/mode changes, loading/error fixtures and long labels.

Frontend acceptance

  • A coach can configure fictional activity prescriptions in their separate planning destination, use all four steps types and see day-effective history in the client view and timeline. The later supplement schedule has its own corresponding client view.
  • A client can complete the training-logbook flow with actual set inputs, contextual notes and history; no duplicate session entry is required in the daily tracker.
  • The same progress-photo comparator works in its intended coach/client contexts with dated images and a usable narrow layout.
  • The tracker displays IIFYM actual-entry controls or the agreed meal-plan accuracy report according to the selected nutrition approach.
  • New client reports can be followed into a local check-in/review/feedback demonstration without contradictory sample values.
  • Required frontend states, responsive behavior and keyboard interactions are implemented and reviewed using fictional data.
  • The handoff documents view-model meanings, component inputs/events, unresolved decisions and service boundaries. Lack of real backend wiring is not a reason to reject the frontend.

Later scope and boundaries

Full food diary and barcode/recipe workflows, advanced logbook tools, richer photo annotation/overlays, detailed video-recording flows and more specialized analytics after the priority views are usable.

Later optional modules

Menses/female-athlete insights, peaking/show-day/contest planning, advanced supplement protocols, bloodwork, full Posing Studio, sport-specific packs, education and community.
