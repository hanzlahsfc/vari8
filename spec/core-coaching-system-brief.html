Core coaching system

A configurable workspace that follows each coach’s methods, connects client logging with plans and check-ins, and supports individual client setups.

Product intent and responsibilities

The product is a configurable coaching system for fitness coaches. It carries forward the value of individually built Google Sheets: each coach can shape the tool around their methods, choose the information they collect and review, and adapt the client experience.

The direction comes from several years of designing custom coaching Google Sheets for more than 100 coaches. The product owner contributes that coaching knowledge and defines how the tool should behave.

A coach builds and saves a client tracking template, assigns it to a client, and then sets that client’s plans, goals and habits. The client logs information, follows the plans and submits check-ins. The coach reviews that information in context, changes plans and delivers feedback.

Central rule: switching the views or variants within a client’s assigned template must preserve the client’s data and avoid requiring a new tracker.

ResponsibilityOwner
Functional direction and coaching logicProduct owner: defines coach needs, client experience, fields, calculations, workflows and examples based on experience building tools for coaches.
Core developmentDevelopment partner: designs and implements the system, including the technical approach and integrations.
Commercial workMarketing and pricing are outside this functional brief and outside the product owner’s stated role.

Primary competitor benchmark: Pono, identified by the product owner as the most important competitor to compare against. This brief records the intended system; it does not reproduce the earlier market report.

Core scope

The current scope centers on the coach workspace, template editor, client plans/logging, check-ins and feedback. Wearable connections, migration and export are part of the intended system.

  • Client roster, profiles and attention summary.
  • Recurring and additional check-in dates, shown in a calendar.
  • Private coach notes/to-dos and separate client-assigned tasks.
  • Template library with multiple views/variants.
  • Configurable dashboards, trackers and weekly roadmap calculations.
  • Client meal plans and native calorie/macro logging.
  • Training programs, logbook notes and set flags.
  • Full and smaller custom check-ins.
  • Integrated messaging and video feedback.
  • Optional habits inside the core client tool.

Standalone sport-specific and menstrual-cycle add-ons are deferred. Show-planning information is one example of content a coach might select for the Timeline/Roadmap; it is not a mandatory roadmap element or a defined competition module.

This is an overview of the intended core build. It does not assign implementation stages, delivery estimates, a technology stack or subscription tiers.

How the system fits together

Coach workspace

The coach’s main workspace contains the roster, check-in calendar, notes/to-dos, template library and messaging. A client profile is the entry point into that client’s assigned tracker, chat, plans and check-in history.

Visibility rule: an internal coach note or coach to-do linked to a client remains part of the coach workspace. A client-assigned task is a different item intended for that client.

Template editor and library

The editor defines the structure of the client tool. Actual meal plans and training programs are populated when the coach sets up the individual client.

Variants within one template: a coach can maintain, for example, a meal-plan-based variant and an IIFYM-based variant. The coach can change the active view/variant at any time while retaining client entries, plans and history.

Optional Habit Tracker: the editor lets the coach select whether the client tool contains this separate feature. Habits have their own setup and logging; they are independent of the ordinary Tracker.

The six core tabs

Dashboard

Configurable summaries and visualizations.

FunctionLocationExpected behavior
D01Template editorBuild the dashboard with an iOS-widget-style grid. Add, position and resize components within that grid.
D02Widget galleryA plus button opens a large library of widgets/visualizations grouped by type, such as nutrition, training, lifestyle and habits.
D03Widget configurationChoose the specific parameters and summaries shown by a widget. Example: a recovery graph uses the recovery fields the coach selects from client logging.
D04Widget sizingPlace a selected widget at its default size. Smaller sizes show a simpler summary; larger sizes show more detail. The content adapts to the size.
D05Assigned client viewPopulate the configured summaries and graphs from that client’s entries and plans.

Coach-facing

Choose the information needed for review and configure the widget layout and its data sources.

Client-facing

Use the dashboard content published for the client. The exact relationship between coach and client dashboard layouts needs a visibility/layout rule.

Resizing changes the level of displayed detail; it does not create a separate source of tracking data.

Tracker

Daily measurements selected by the coach.

FunctionLocationExpected behavior
T01Parameter libraryOffer a broad list of available parameters grouped by type. Examples: bodyweight → weight and weigh-in time; health → resting heart rate (RHR), fasting blood glucose (FBG) and blood pressure (BP).
T02Template editorThe coach selects which parameters appear in the tracker.
T03General configuration menuSelect bodyweight and other general units, plus the rating scheme: 1–10, 1–5 or word labels.
T04Client TrackerSupport easy daily entry on mobile and desktop. Relevant entries feed dashboards, roadmap summaries and check-ins.

Coach-facing

Configure the visible parameters and review the athlete’s daily data. Unit and rating settings must be applied consistently across the connected views.

Client-facing

Enter the selected measurements using the configured input controls and units. The client-facing tracker should make daily logging straightforward.

Timeline / Roadmap

A weekly overview built from coach-selected parameters and calculations.

The Timeline/Roadmap is a configurable weekly progress overview. The coach freely chooses which parameters, summaries and planning information to display, using a selection approach similar to the Tracker.

Configuration rule: the roadmap is built from the coach’s selection. Bodyweight, nutrition, activity and the other examples below do not define a fixed set of roadmap fields or an exhaustive list of available options.

FunctionLocationExpected behavior
R01Template editor / parameter selectionLet the coach choose the roadmap parameters and metrics from the available options, as they choose fields for the Tracker. The roadmap content follows this selection.
R02Calculation selectionLet the coach choose which supported calculations or summaries to show for the selected parameters, such as weekly averages, trends, target-versus-actual comparisons or adherence percentages where applicable.
R03Planning selectionLet the coach include the supported planning information relevant to their methods. Planning fields and comparisons are optional choices rather than fixed content in every roadmap.
R04Assigned client viewPopulate the configured weekly overview from that client’s relevant data, targets and plans. Display the information selected by the coach.

Illustrative choices — not fixed roadmap fields

These are the product owner’s examples of what a coach might select. They illustrate the flexibility of the roadmap; they do not define the complete selection library.

Example parameter / contentPossible display or calculation, if selected

Planned bodyweightUse a start date as an anchor, plan percentages for rate of gain (ROG) or rate of loss (ROL), and show the planned weight for each week.
Actual bodyweightShow weekly average bodyweight and actual ROG/ROL, with a comparison to the planned trend if that comparison is selected.

Coach-facing

Select the roadmap parameters and choose their supported summaries, calculations and planning information to match the coach’s review process.

Client-facing

View the weekly overview configured by the coach, with the selected information made available to the client.

The range of available parameters and calculations, aggregation windows and visibility rules need detailed definitions. The example list above does not restrict the roadmap to those choices.

Mealplan / food tracking

Individual meal plans, flexible nutrition targets and logging.

The Mealplan tab uses built-in client setup rather than a meal-plan structure customized in the template editor. The coach configures actual plans for the individual athlete.

FunctionLocationExpected behavior
N01Client meal-plan setupChoose meal count, meal labels, times and frequency. Provide approved food swaps and equivalent portions.
N02TargetsSupport custom macro goals or calorie-only goals for individual meals, as well as daily total goals.
N03Target optionsPrescribe calories/macros as fixed values, ranges or minimums. Use a nutrition-variant schedule or manual selection.
N04Food loggingSearch foods, scan barcodes, add custom foods and quick-add calories/macros.
N05Reusable food entriesSave recipes/favorites, copy previous meals or days and adjust servings.
N06Daily totalsShow consumed and remaining nutrients against the applicable variant’s targets.
N07Coach reviewMake the client’s food entries and consumption available to the coach and to selected weekly/check-in summaries.
N08Plan variants / historyRetain the earlier core direction: one meal-plan option by default, additional options with coach-defined labels such as training/rest day or high/medium/low day, a selected start date, and archived older meal plans.

Coach-facing

Set the client’s meal structure, targets, swaps and nutrition schedule. Review actual food entries and change the plan during check-in review.

Client-facing

Follow the assigned meal plan or use the enabled IIFYM logging experience, record intake and see the totals relevant to the current targets.

Calorie/macro and food-diary requirements are grouped here even where they appeared under the “training” heading in the supplied notes. Labeled plan options and archived plans are carried forward from the earlier core direction.

Training Program / logbook

Evergreen or week-based programming, volume views and contextual notes.

FunctionLocationExpected behavior
P01Template editorChoose the exercise information and prescription fields the coach wants to use, such as tempo, set type and rest/pause time. Set default progression notes/system.
P02Volume displayChoose whether volume is shown/calculated, the extent of the breakdown, and whether the display is a table or heatmap.
P03Client training setupWrite the actual training program for the individual client using the enabled prescription fields. Earlier training direction includes fixed reps/rep ranges and detailed exercise instructions.
P04Client logbookLet the client write notes against a specific set and optionally flag the note for check-in review. Retain exercise/setup notes from the earlier core direction.
P05Future coach notesAllow the coach to write a note on a future set, such as “reduce weight and focus on form”, for the client to see at the relevant point.
P06Review contextOpen logged performance and flagged notes from the client’s check-in review context.
P07Client training setup / program typeLet the coach choose an evergreen program or a week-based program. Evergreen programming is ongoing without a preset week count. For a week-based program, the coach selects the number of weeks.
P08Week-based programming / carry-forward instructionsThe coach sets the first week’s instructions. Each following week mirrors the previous week until the coach makes a change. A change applies from the chosen week onward and continues through later weeks until another change is made; earlier weeks retain their instructions.

Coach-facing

Configure the available prescription fields, choose evergreen or week-based programming, and select the week count when applicable. Write the initial instructions and introduce changes at the intended week without re-entering unchanged instructions for every week. Review performance and leave instructions for future sets.

Client-facing

Follow the evergreen program or the instructions applicable to the current week of the assigned week-based program, and use the training logbook. Set notes retain their exercise/set context, and flagged notes are visible to the coach during review.

Week-based example: leg extension

The coach selects the program’s week count, then sets three leg-extension sets in week 1. That prescription carries forward until the coach adds a set in week 4.

WeekCoach actionResulting prescription
Week 1Prescribe 3 sets of leg extension.3 sets.
Weeks 2–3No change.3 sets, carried forward from week 1.
Week 4Change the prescription to 4 sets.4 sets from this week.
Week 5 through the final selected weekNo further change.4 sets, carried forward from week 4 until another change is made.

The meaning of volume, its allocation and exercise progression calculations still need coaching definitions. The carry-forward behavior described above is the specified rule for week-based program instructions.

C01Template editorBuild the check-in questionnaire with a form builder.
C02Included summariesSelect which averages and statuses are displayed/submitted, such as average bodyweight, nutrition, habits and sleep.

Client-facing

Choose a full or smaller custom submission, share the relevant update and receive the coach’s feedback and links to updated plans.

A custom check-in provides a lightweight mid-week update without requiring the full weekly questionnaire. Its relationship to coach-requested metrics and scheduled dates needs a defined rule.

Habit Tracker

The Habit Tracker is an optional, independent feature within the client tool. It has its own habit setup and status logging, with no connection to the ordinary Tracker’s measurement fields.

Once it is included in a template, the coach defines the client’s actual habits, frequency, goals and the reason behind each habit so the client understands its purpose.

FunctionCoach-facingClient-facing
Include the featureChoose whether the template includes the Habit Tracker.Access the separate Habit Tracker when it is enabled.
Habits and frequencyDefine the habits the client should follow and how often.See each habit and its intended frequency.
Goals and purposeSpecify the goal and reason behind each habit.Understand what to aim for and why the habit matters.
Habit statusReview the client’s recorded habit status.Record habit status within the Habit Tracker.

Habit definitions belong to individual client setup. Habit statuses can be selected separately for dashboard widgets and check-in summaries. Client-assigned tasks and internal coach to-dos are covered in the Coach workspace section.

Messaging and feedback

Provide a simple WhatsApp-like coach–client messaging interface, with filters for unread and flagged messages.

FunctionLocationExpected behavior
M01ChatSend messages, voice notes, files, photos and videos.
M02Context linksLink a message to a workout, meal or check-in.
M03Coach controlsDefine availability and notification preferences. Provide conversation search and reusable reply snippets.

Messaging, training notes and check-in feedback should retain their context even when the coach changes the visible template variant. The exact location of the client’s feedback history can be decided during detailed screen design.

Wearables, migration and export

Wearable connections

Support a connector direction for devices/services such as Apple Watch, Fitbit and Oura Ring. Bring agreed measurements into relevant client tracking fields so they can be used by selected dashboard, weekly and check-in summaries.

The first supported providers, imported measurements, synchronization behavior and handling of manual entries need to be specified with the development partner.

Migration and export

Provide a path to migrate existing coach/client data into the platform and export usable records out of it. Existing custom Google Sheets are an important source context for migration.

Define which fields, historical entries, plans, formulas/configuration and attachments are included. The notes do not yet specify import/export formats or how much migration is automatic.
